## Candidate status and migration

This is a release candidate, not `v0.1.0` or a production SLA commitment.
It introduces the Genesis v3 admission contract and retains the active-inclusive HTTP quota introduced in Genesis v2.
Use the CLI, chart, controller, runner, and worker from this exact release together; record the published immutable role digests in deployment evidence.

### Changes since rc.10

- Genesis v3 pins the external worker-pool identity and runner OCI manifest/index digest, and records immutable queue-reservation, active-grant, and Pod-Create receipts before their effects.
  Receipt uncertainty, identity drift, a committed lost ledger, or an unresolved Pod Create remains charged and fails closed instead of reconstructing capacity from an empty List.
- Active and queue ledgers enforce supported counts, strict identity/schema validation, separate 768 KiB encoded-state guards, and cleanup headroom.
  Cancellation and normal Pod deletion qualify the original UID; a same-name replacement is not adopted.
- HTTP admission, build reconciliation, leadership renewal, and readiness use explicit traffic-class Kubernetes budgets rather than one implicit client-go bottleneck.
  Telemetry separates limiter wait, bounded wire-attempt counts/RTT, HTTP submission latency, and reconcile time; those observations are not a throughput SLA.
- Finite collection, held name-occupancy, and exact-disposal operator stages are available in the separate, opt-in `kova-recovery` source-built tool.
  They do not sign evidence, retire remote BuildKit work, release old capacity, create a successor, or switch an ingress route.
- Drain-permit signature allocation is bounded before allocation; the CodeQL allocation-size-overflow fix is included.
- Bounded acceptance tools qualify official Kubernetes ledger conflict/delete responses and BuildKit history descriptors, while retaining payload-free diagnostics for uncertain observations.
  Tool compatibility fixes do not retrospectively turn an earlier failed or unknown campaign into a pass.

### Required upgrade procedure

Do not perform a live rolling upgrade from rc.10 or any earlier admission contract.
Stop new submissions, let old builds reach a documented terminal or deleted state, directly confirm that old runner Pods are gone, and stop every old Service/controller writer before starting this candidate.
Retain the old ledgers, immutable receipts, terminal results, and operator evidence outside the old runner and receipt namespaces.
Apply this release's CRD and verify its status-field round trips before Helm upgrade; Helm does not upgrade files in a chart's `crds/` directory.

Provision a never-used runner namespace and a distinct never-used recovery-receipt namespace through the reviewed Genesis installer.
Pin their exact UIDs, the cluster identity, worker-pool identity, runner OCI manifest/index reference, immutable installation receipt, and identical limits on all Service replicas.
Upgrade controller, runner, and worker together, then verify readiness and the new route before resuming submissions.
Never delete a ledger to reset capacity, copy in-flight old CRs into the new namespace, reuse old namespace names, or reduce limits on one replica while work remains.
If an old workload cannot drain or an effect has an unknown outcome, quarantine it and use the separately reviewed incident procedure; a fresh namespace alone is not proof that shared worker capacity is free.

Follow the [release gates](../releases.md), [Service admission contract](../service-admission-design.md), and [committed-loss recovery boundary](../recovery-drain.md) at this tag.

### Supported limits and operational caveats

- `maxActiveJobs`: 1–128; `workerSlots`: 1–65535; `maxQueuedJobs`: 1–1000.
  Each per-requester limit must be positive and no greater than its global limit.
- `maxQueuedJobs` counts all outstanding HTTP-submitted builds, including active builds, until qualified terminal/receipt cleanup releases their charges.
  With one active HTTP build and a limit of 1000, at most 999 such builds can be waiting; direct/admin CRs are outside the HTTP queue quota but still consume active admission capacity.
- The count and encoded-state limits are safety boundaries, not certified workload throughput or a promise that 128 active builds fit a worker pool.
  Begin at modest concurrency and review worker resources, phase timings, cleanup delay, and summed API budgets across Service replicas before increasing load.
- An uncertain receipt, CR, Pod, or ledger write can intentionally leave a charged reservation requiring operator review.
  There is no time-based lease expiry, orphan-intent scavenger, destructive recovery endpoint, or permission to replay an unknown effect under a new idempotency key.
- The committed-loss tools are finite stages, not a completed automatic incident-closure procedure.
  External retirement of Service/runner/worker/network effects, complete outside-namespace archival readback, persistent old-name non-reuse, one-successor allocation, and route UID/resourceVersion CAS remain owner obligations.
  `kova-recovery` is not bundled with ordinary client release artifacts and is never invoked by Helm startup or the normal Service API.
- This candidate does not claim a completed real-GC, full fault-injection, cross-epoch retirement, or long-duration production acceptance matrix.
  A green short smoke and deterministic safety tests do not prove those wider properties; unknown earlier fixtures stay retained for investigation.

Stable `v0.1.0` remains a separate promotion decision based on target-environment evidence.
